SECTION 1

What a Vase and Vase Block Are

A permanent place for flowers that the mowers can pass over

A cemetery vase isn't a vase you set on the grass. It sits in a hole cut into a granite block that's set level with the lawn. When it's holding flowers, it stands upright. When it isn't, it turns upside down and rests flush in the block, so the mowers pass right over it and nothing gets broken.

Every option in this guide uses the same 12 by 12 by 4 inch granite block, polished on top with sawn sides, with the hole for the vase already drilled. Our own crew sets the block and the vase.

If your loved one has a bronze marker, it can carry its own vase core, the fitting a vase screws into, and the vase can go in the marker instead. The Bronze Markers guide covers that. This guide is about a vase on its own block.

WHAT YOU'RE CHOOSING
  • The vase: gray zinc, or one of three bronze styles
  • The block: the granite color, which sets its price group
  • Setting it: one installation charge, $175

SECTION 5

Why They Cost What They Do

It's the most common question families ask about vases, and it deserves a straight answer

Bronze is mostly copper. The bronze vases are solid castings by Matthews, and bronze is a copper alloy. Copper prices are at record highs, and the cost of every bronze casting follows them. That's the biggest single reason a bronze vase costs what it does today.

Bronze lasts for generations. Pacific Coast Memorials describes bronze as an extremely durable copper-based alloy, and it doesn't rust. Zinc costs far less and outlasts galvanized metal, but by their own description its life is centuries shorter than bronze. Families who choose bronze buy it once.

The block is memorial-grade granite. Pacific Coast Memorials, a fifth-generation family company in Everett, cuts each block, polishes the top and drills it for the vase. As they put it, the price of granite depends on its size and the type of granite. Every vase block here is the same size, so the color is what moves the price, and some colors carry added import tariffs.

It's backed by a warranty. Pacific Coast Memorials will replace or refurbish, at no cost to you, any stone part that cracks or breaks down from climate or season. Normal weathering stains aren't covered.

It's set to stay. The price includes our crew setting the block level so it stays put and the mowers can pass over it for as long as the cemetery stands.

If the bronze price is the hesitation

The gray zinc vase on a Group 1 block comes to $1,120.56 with tax, about half the cost of bronze. It's a real, permanent vase that meets every cemetery rule. Plenty of families start there.

SECTION 6

The Cemetery's Flower Rules

Why a permanent vase matters, from the Washington Memorial Park rules and regulations

Fresh cut flowers are welcome at any time, in a regulation vase. Without a vase, there's no regulation place for them, and flowers left on the grass are removed by our grounds crew when they become unsightly.

Non-permanent vases aren't allowed. That includes plastic and glass vases. Permanent vases are purchased through the office and installed by the park. Glass is the bigger concern: it can shatter under a mower and injure someone.

Artificial flowers are allowed only during the non-mowing season. Any placed during mowing season are removed and disposed of.

Holiday decorations come down 7 to 14 days after each holiday.

NOT PERMITTED ON A GRAVE
  • Plastic or other non-permanent vases
  • Glass of any kind
  • Balloons and pinwheels
  • Planter boxes and decorative rocks
  • Photographs and souvenirs
  • Preserved flowers
  • Enclosures or borders around a plot
