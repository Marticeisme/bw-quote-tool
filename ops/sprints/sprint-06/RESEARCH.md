## Part 2 — Claims to avoid or soften

Ordered roughly by how much damage getting it wrong would do in front of a family.

### 2.1 ⚠ The soil weight — a straight 2× contradiction inside BW's own materials

- `[BW-DESC]`: "By weight, this represents about **500 pounds** of earth."
- `[RH-TRAIN]` p7: "Typically, the family will receive approximately **250 lbs.** of soil."
  — and it gives a mechanism: "in addition to the body, around 200 lbs. of organics are
  added to the vessel."
- `[RH-TRAIN]` p3 independently repeats "approximately **250 pounds** of soil."

Both agree on ~1 cubic yard of *volume*. The internal consistency check favours 250 lb: the
booklet's own bag table `[BW-BOOK]` p7 maxes at 20 lb per large bag, and its FAQ says the
full amount is "10-15 burlap bags full" — 10–15 × 20 lb ≈ 200–300 lb, which matches 250 and
not 500.

**Do not print a weight in the guide until Martice confirms it.** Print the volume (~1 cubic
yard) and the bag counts, both of which every source agrees on. A family who is told 500 lb
and receives 250 lb has been mis-set on the single most physically concrete part of the
service.

### 2.2 ⚠ The timeline — three different answers

| Source | Says |
|---|---|
| `[BW-DESC]` | 30 days in vessel + 30 days curing = "the 60 day process" |
| `[S3]` Return Home immediate-need page | "60 days in total" — 30 + 30 |
| `[BW-BOOK]` p3 | phase 1 is "just **30–40 days**", phase 2 "the next 30 days" (implies 60–70) |
| `[RH-TRAIN]` p3 | "In total, it takes approximately **9-10 weeks**" (63–70 days) |
| `[S5]` Return Home Auburn page | "approximately **60–90 days**" |

The brief's premise ("60 days total") is supported, but **Return Home's own site is internally
inconsistent** — one page says 60, another says 60–90. Safest guide language: *"about two
months — roughly 30 days in the vessel and about another 30 days of resting"*, phrased as an
approximation, never as a promise of a date. Do **not** write "60 days" as a guarantee.

### 2.3 ⚠ "Burns no green-house gasses" — this one is not defensible

`[BW-DESC]` states: "Unlike the cremation process, Terramation burns no green-house gasses".
Terramation is aerobic decomposition: it *produces* CO₂ by definition, and the facility runs
an HVAC/air-scrubbing system on electricity. The defensible claim is about **fossil-fuel
combustion and net footprint**, not zero emissions.

What can actually be said, with attribution:

- Cremation burns fossil fuel to hold >1,600 °F for 3–4 hours; NOR does not. `[S14]`
- Recompose's figures — **87% less energy than cremation**, and **0.84–1.4 metric tons of CO₂
  saved per person** versus cremation — come from a model by **Dr. Troy Hottle**. `[S14]`,
  `[S15]`
- **Hottle is credentialed** (PhD, Arizona State; senior LCA analyst at Eastern Research
  Group) and has a peer-reviewed publication record in LCA generally. `[S15]` **But I could
  not confirm that the human-composting LCA itself was independently peer-reviewed or
  published in a journal, and it is presented on Recompose's own site — i.e. a provider's
  commissioned model.** Say so or don't cite the number.

**Recommendation:** the guide should make a *directional* environmental claim ("uses
substantially less energy than cremation and creates usable soil rather than releasing that
carbon as exhaust"), attribute any percentage to its source, and drop "burns no green-house
gasses" entirely. It is the kind of sentence a well-read family member will challenge in the
arrangement room.

Also note: `[S5]` Return Home's "considerably less energy than burial or cremation" is a
vendor claim about itself, unquantified.

### 2.4 ⚠ The Woodland cannot be presented as a place to visit

`[RH-TRAIN]` p7 is explicit: "visitations to the Woodland are not permitted, as it is neither
staffed nor supervised." Return Home's public Woodland page `[S6]` says none of this — it
calls it "a peaceful resting place" and does not mention the restriction. A guide that borrows
the public page's language will imply visiting, and a family who chooses Woodland donation on
that basis has been misled about the one thing that matters to them.

If the Woodland appears in the guide at all, it must say the land is a protected greenbelt
that is **not open for visits**, and may offer the Soos Creek walking trail alongside it as
what families actually do `[RH-TRAIN]` p7. Note that the Soos Creek suggestion is also
single-sourced to the internal doc — verify with Return Home before printing it.

**This is also the strongest argument for the WMP placement option** (§2.9 / Part 3), which
*is* visitable and *is* BW's own ground.

### 2.5 ⚠ Do not print Return Home's $5,950 anywhere near BW's $7,795

Both figures are real and both are published `[S7]`, `[GPL]` p13. They measure different
things: BW's includes BW's basic services of funeral director and staff ($2,425 standalone,
`[GPL]` p9), transfer to the funeral home, the bathing, and the utility vehicle, plus the NOR
"through a third-party". Return Home's is the NOR provider's own direct-to-family price.

But a family reading both side by side sees a $1,845 markup and no explanation. **The guide
should quote BW's $7,795 and what it includes — the GPL's own inclusion sentence does the
work — and not reproduce Return Home's price list.** Whether $7,795 is fully inclusive of
Return Home's charge is an open question (Part 3).

### 2.6 ⚠ "Washington was the first place in the world"

`[BW-BOOK]` p9 claims Washington was "the first place in the **world**" to legalize
terramation. What is verified is that it was **the first US state** `[S8]`, `[S9]`, `[S10]`.
The global claim is plausible but I found no primary source establishing it, and it is the
kind of superlative that is easy to say and hard to defend. **Soften to "the first state in
the United States"** — which is impressive, true, and citable.

Same page also states discussions began in 2017 and the bill was proposed by 2018 with full
legal approval "by May of that year", which does not match the verified timeline (signed
2019-05-21, effective 2020-05-01). Treat `[BW-BOOK]` p9's chronology as unreliable and use the
Legislature's dates.

### 2.7 ⚠ Effective-date conflict

`[S8]` (WA Legislature bill summary) and `[S9]` (MRSC, a Washington municipal-research body)
both say the NOR provisions took effect **May 1, 2020**. `[S10]` (Recompose) says **May 7,
2020**. **Use May 1, 2020**, or simply write "2020" — the precise day carries no weight for a
family and the conflict is not worth inheriting.

### 2.8 ⚠ The eligibility list is not fully verified

`[BW-BOOK]` p10 lists: prion illness (e.g. Creutzfeldt-Jakob), highly infectious disease
(e.g. Ebola), prior embalming, and **weight over 500 lb**. Independent sourcing `[S13]`
corroborates prion disease, Ebola and prior embalming, **adds active tuberculosis**, and adds
that radiation seed implants must be removed with 30 days elapsed. **No source I found
corroborates the 500 lb limit** — it is likely a Return Home vessel-capacity constraint rather
than a general rule.

For a family guide, the honest and kind framing is *"a small number of medical circumstances
rule terramation out — your family service director will check"*, rather than a list. A printed
list that is wrong in either direction (omitting TB, or stating a weight limit that turns out
to be different) causes a hard conversation later. Confirm the current list with Return Home
before printing any of it.

### 2.9 ⚠ The bag sizes disagree

`[BW-BOOK]` p7 (family-facing): 20 lb / 10 lb / 2 lb / mini handful.
`[RH-TRAIN]` p7 (internal): "small sachets, 2 lb., 5 lb., and 20 lb."

Prefer the booklet — it is the family-facing artefact, it is BW-branded, and it is more
recent-looking. But flag it: if the guide prints a 10 lb bag and Return Home ships a 5 lb bag,
that is a complaint.

### 2.10 ⚠ Vendor-only claims, usable but must be attributed or dropped

- "World's first large-scale Terramation center", "74 vessels" `[S2]` — Return Home about itself.
- "Voted 'Washington State Funeral Home of the Year' and 'Best of the Best' in the United
  States in its first year of operation" `[BW-BOOK]` p9 — no awarding body named, no year
  verified. **Drop it** unless Martice can name the award.
- "Roughly 60% of our families choose to take the full amount of soil home" `[RH-TRAIN]` p7 —
  internal sales figure. Do not print.
- "Uses no chemicals or inoculants" `[BW-DESC]`, "our vessels do not contain anything that
  will reduce or alter the body in any way" `[BW-BOOK]` p4 — plausible and consistent with the
  three-organics description, but provider-attested. Fine to state as *"Return Home uses only
  straw, alfalfa and sawdust — no chemicals"*, attributed.
- "The fastest-growing new disposition option in America" `[BW-BOOK]` p9 — no data source.
  Soften to "a growing option" or drop.

### 2.11 ⚠ The 14-state list is aggregator-sourced

`[S12]` is a search aggregation over advocacy and industry trackers, not a check of 14
statutes. States are also actively legislating (Rhode Island pending as of May 2026). **If the
guide names states at all, date-stamp the sentence** ("as of mid-2026, about a dozen states")
rather than printing a list that goes stale on the shelf. Better: say Washington was first and
that other states have since followed, without enumerating.

### 2.12 ⚠ Religious perspectives — true, and delicate

The **USCCB Committee on Doctrine issued a statement on 2023-03-23** finding that both
alkaline hydrolysis and human composting "fail to satisfy the Church's requirements for proper
respect for the bodies of the dead", holding that they do not show adequate respect for the
body nor express hope in the resurrection. The Church holds burial as most fitting and permits
cremation with conditions. `[S16]`, `[S17]`

This is **real, current, and from the bishops themselves** — it is not a rumour, and a Catholic
family may well raise it.

Against that, `[RH-TRAIN]` p5 states that clergy of various faiths including rabbis and priests
do lead laying-in ceremonies, and `[RH-TRAIN]` p11 makes a point of terramation's appeal
across "diverse political, demographic and religious backgrounds".

**Recommendation:** do not put a "Religious perspectives" section in a family guide. It cannot
be done briefly without either misrepresenting the USCCB position or appearing to argue with
it, and the guide is a sales-adjacent document. The right handling is: **the counselor knows
this fact and answers it honestly if asked**, and the guide stays silent. If Martice wants it
addressed in print, it needs his decision and probably his own words — flagged in Part 3.

Note also that no equivalent position was researched for other traditions (Jewish, Muslim,
Orthodox Christian, Buddhist, Hindu). **Do not generalise from the Catholic finding**, and do
not write anything implying broad religious acceptance either — `[RH-TRAIN]` p11's
cross-demographic framing is a sales assertion, not evidence.

### 2.13 ⚠ Nothing from `[RH-TRAIN]` Section 5 may appear in the guide

The "Terramation Affinity Test", the prospect archetypes ("Eco-Advocates", "Freedom
Guardians", "Alternative Option Seekers"), the scripted opening question, and all five quizzes
are internal sales-training material. Several would read as manipulative if a family saw them.
They are recorded here only so the guide track knows to recognise and exclude them.
