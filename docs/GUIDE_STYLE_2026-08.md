# Guide Style Reference, August 2026

**Produced by:** sprint-24 Track R (`s24/style-research`)
**For:** every track that rewrites a family-facing guide
**Read first, and treat as binding:** `docs/GUIDES_VOICE_DEBRIEF_2026-08.md`

This is a distilled reference, not a rulebook of its own. It samples the best published
writing about death care, pulls out what those writers actually do, and then runs every
technique back through Martice's voice rules. Where a journalism technique and a voice
rule disagree, **the voice rule wins.** That is stated again at each point where it
matters.

Scope note: this file describes prose. It says nothing about layout, brand tokens, or the
card contract. Those live in `docs/BRAND_AND_BUILD_LOG.md`.

## 1. Sources sampled

Fifteen pieces, all fetched and read on 2026-08-20. Twelve consumer-facing, three trade.
Quotations are held to one short fragment per source, attributed, per the copyright rule
in the track brief.

### Consumer-facing

**1. Funeral Consumers Alliance, "Ten Tips for Saving Funeral Dollars"**
https://www.funerals.org/get-help/how-to-save-money/ten-tips-for-saving-funeral-dollars/
Opens by naming the reader's real goal (spend less) and refusing to treat that goal as
shameful. Prices appear as bare figures with no apology around them. The weakness is
pace: the brisk imperative "shop around!" reads as a consumer-advocacy voice, not a
person sitting with you, and lands badly on someone whose father died on Tuesday.

**2. Funeral Consumers Alliance, "Your Funeral Rights"**
https://www.funerals.org/your-rights/ftc-funeral-rule/your-funeral-rights/
Best single technique in the sample: it converts every prohibition into something the
reader can do. "You are not required to purchase a package" becomes a statement about
what the reader may choose instead. Weakness: it drifts into regulatory density and
assumes the reader already knows terms like basic services fee.

**3. Funeral Consumers Alliance, "FTC Funeral Rule"**
https://www.funerals.org/your-rights/ftc-funeral-rule/
Explains a federal regulation by leading with what it lets you do rather than what it
forbids: the rule "makes it possible for you to choose only those goods and services you
want" (Funeral Consumers Alliance). Weakness: the first two sentences run past forty
words each, which is exactly the sentence length a distressed reader cannot hold.

**4. Funeral Consumers Alliance, "How to Pay for a Funeral or Other Arrangements"**
https://www.funerals.org/get-help/how-to-save-money/how-to-pay-for-a-funeral-or-other-arrangements/
The hardest-nosed piece in the sample and the most useful model for landing an
unwelcome fact. It opens by telling the reader plainly that nobody is coming to cover
the bill, then immediately gives a real price range to plan against. No cushion, no
euphemism, and no cruelty either. Weakness: it warns readers off prepaying without
distinguishing the cases where prepaying is the right call.

**5. Modern Loss, "Funeral Planning: What You Need To Know"**
https://modernloss.com/funeral-planning-what-you-need-to-know/
Names the reader's state before giving instructions: the logistics after a death are
called an "onslaught," and the piece calls those tasks the "insults to injury"
(Modern Loss). One sentence of recognition, then work. Weakness: the empathy preamble
runs longer than it needs to, and a reader who came for a checklist has to wade.

**6. Modern Loss, "It's Your Funeral: 10 Ways to Hack the Planning"**
https://modernloss.com/funeral-10-ways-hack-planning/
Written by a sixth-generation funeral director, which makes it the closest analogue to
Martice's own position: an insider telling families what to watch for. Every tip carries
a dollar figure, including a same-service price spread between two funeral homes.
Weakness: the irreverence is calibrated for that outlet's readership, and the numbered
listicle shape encourages skimming past the reasoning.

**7. Modern Loss, "Mourning the Dad Who Walked Out on Me"**
https://modernloss.com/mourning-dad-walked/
A personal essay, sampled to see how a hard fact gets landed without softening. It says
the father left, that he did not call and did not write, in short flat sentences with no
qualifying clause. That flatness is the technique. Weakness for our purposes: the whole
piece is one person's meaning-making, which does not generalize to a guide.

**8. The Order of the Good Death, "Everything You Need to Get Started"**
https://www.orderofthegooddeath.com/start-here/
Useful as a boundary marker. The voice is warm and funny about mortality, but the piece
puts a critique of the funeral industry ahead of any practical help. A family already
sitting in an arrangement conference is being told the room they are in is the problem.
Sampled mainly for what NOT to import.

**9. Gothamist, "Compost your corpse: Brooklyn's Green-Wood Cemetery to make dying more
green"**
https://gothamist.com/news/compost-your-corpse-brooklyns-green-wood-cemetery-to-make-dying-more-green
Straight news. Hard numbers land in a row with no throat-clearing: batch size, day count,
acreage, a gravesite price. That density is worth stealing. The news hook, the
third-person distance, and the joke in the headline are not.

**10. The Conversation, "Boom in cremation hides surprising truths about what Americans
really want when they die"**
https://theconversation.com/boom-in-cremation-hides-surprising-truths-about-what-americans-really-want-when-they-die-280340
Opens with a fact the reader already half-knows, then complicates it. The author names
her own standing in one short sentence before proceeding, which buys authority cheaply
and honestly. Weakness for us: the whole structure is contrarian, built to tell the
reader their assumption is wrong, which is the shape of our banned opener.

**11. TIME, "Cremation: The New American Way of Death"**
https://time.com/3800220/cremation-the-new-american-way-of-death/
A photo essay. Its opening is genuinely lovely, built on repetition about grandparents
and great-grandparents being buried. It is also the clearest example of a flourish that
must not be imported: rhythm is doing the work that information should do.

**12. Funeralwise, "Working with a Funeral Director: The Arrangement Conference"**
https://www.funeralwise.com/funeral-arrangements/funeral-conference/
The closest thing in the sample to what our guides are. It tells a family what the
meeting will be like before they walk into it, in short second-person sentences. That
demystification is the single most transferable move here. Weakness: it declines to
discuss money frankly and assumes the family agrees with itself.

### Trade, sampled for contrast

**13. Connecting Directors, "The Funeral Profession: Expectations Vs. Reality"**
https://connectingdirectors.com/45019-the-funeral-profession-expectations-vs-reality
Warm first-person shop talk aimed at other directors. Reads as peers comparing notes, and
assumes the reader already understands industry stereotypes and industry pay. The tone is
likable and would still be wrong in front of a family, because it is familiar rather than
warm, and it is about us rather than about them.

**14. NFDA, "Decoding the Evolving Mindset of Funeral Consumers"**
https://www.nfda.org/decoding-the-evolving-mindset-of-funeral-consumers-at-new-nfda-consumer-trends-conference/
Opens with a collage of unattributed family quotes, then turns to market intelligence.
The word "consumer" is doing heavy lifting throughout. This is the register the debrief
is objecting to when it flags marketing language: families are a market to be understood,
not people to be told something.

**15. American Funeral Director (via the publisher's reprint), "Connecting Within Your
Community"**
https://thumbies.com/from-american-funeral-director-thumbies-connecting-within-your-community/
Bridges from a real human observation about memorialization into an industry "we," then
into business terms. It is the exact seam where brochure copy is born. Worth reading once
so you can recognize the move and never make it.

## 2. Opening moves that work

The good pieces open in one of four ways. All four share a property: **the first sentence
carries information the reader can use, and none of them are about the article.**

**a. Start with the fact the reader is most afraid of.** FCA's payment guide opens by
telling you that you or your family are paying this bill and no charity is coming. It is
the worst news in the piece and it goes first, which means everything after it reads as
help rather than as a set-up.

**b. Start with the single thing that has to happen next.** The Funeralwise arrangement
piece and our own Burial Guide both do this. One action, stated flatly, before any
context.

**c. Start with a number.** Gothamist and The Conversation both open on a figure the
reader can hold. A number is a promise that the rest of the piece will also be specific.

**d. Start with a plain definition of the thing being decided.** No adjectives, no
significance, just what it is.

**What none of them do:** announce that the article is important, rank the article's own
contents, characterize the reader's likely ignorance, or clear a throat. See §7.

## 3. Cadence and paragraph craft

**Vary sentence length deliberately, and put the short one where the weight is.** The
pattern in every good piece sampled is the same: a medium sentence sets up, a short one
lands. "My dad left when I was nine" is nine words and carries the essay. FCA's "No
charity will bail you out" is the same move applied to money.

**One idea per paragraph, and stop when the idea is done.** Three sentences is a normal
paragraph. Two is fine. The weakest passages in the sample are all the ones where a
paragraph kept going after its point had arrived.

**Land a hard fact without a cushion and without a flinch.** This is the hardest thing to
copy and the most valuable. The technique is not bluntness, it is sequence: state the
hard fact in a short sentence, then immediately give the reader something to do about it.
FCA states that nobody will cover the bill, then gives a price range. The move is *fact,
then footing*. What it is not is a euphemism, a softening adverb, or an appeal to
dignity. "Cremated remains are buried in the ground" beats "lovingly returned to the
earth," and the debrief already flags that exact phrase.

**Definitions belong inline, at the moment of first use.** Our own Who Decides guide does
this with RCW 68.50.160 and it is better than anything in the outside sample: it names
the law, then explains that RCW just means the state's collection of laws and that the
number is an address. Two sentences, no jargon left standing.

**Second person, and stay in it.** Every strong consumer piece addresses "you." The ones
that slip into "families" or "consumers" get colder in the same sentence.

## 4. Trust techniques

Ranked by how much trust they buy per sentence.

**1. Name the price.** Not a range where a number exists, not "affordable," not "starting
at." Every trustworthy piece in the sample prints figures. Vagueness about money is read
as a sales posture, correctly.

**2. Tell the reader what they do not have to buy.** FCA does this three separate ways
and it is the reason that site is trusted. It costs a seller very little and it is the
fastest way to stop sounding like one.

**3. Admit the trade-off in the same breath as the recommendation.** The good pieces
never present an option as having no downside. Our Cremation or Burial guide already does
this well by refusing to steer.

**4. Say plainly where a rule comes from, and invite the reader to check it.** Who Decides
tells the reader to go read the statute and check us against it. That single sentence is
worth more than a page of reassurance.

**5. State your own position.** The Conversation's author says what she is in one clause.
The Modern Loss director says he is a sixth-generation funeral director in his first
sentence. Martice's equivalent already exists and is honest: he sells this, he works
here, and he is telling you how it works anyway.

**6. Explain why a charge exists, not just that it does.** The debrief asks for this
explicitly on the outside-marker inspection fee. It is also the single most common thing
missing from trade and vendor writing.

## 5. What NOT to import

- **Third-person distance.** "Families often find..." is the register of a report about
  people. Our guides are addressed to one person.
- **News hooks and topicality.** Journalism opens on what is new. A guide opens on what is
  true. Nothing in a guide should decay because a month passed.
- **Investigative adversarialism.** The Order of the Good Death and, more mildly, FCA
  frame the industry as the opposing party. Martice IS the industry, and he is being
  honest inside it. Importing an adversarial stance would be both dishonest and
  self-defeating: a guide that implies the reader is in a hostile room is a guide written
  by someone who is in that room with them.
- **Literary flourish.** The TIME opening is beautiful and would be wrong here. Anaphora,
  cadence for its own sake, and imagery are all ways of making the reader feel something
  the information has not earned yet.
- **The listicle.** "10 ways to hack" is scannable and shallow. Our guides already use
  numbered sections, which is different: those are structure, not a hook.
- **Humor about death.** It works for Modern Loss's readership because that readership
  opted in. A family in an appointment did not.
- **"Consumer," "market," "product offering," "memorialization."** Trade vocabulary. It
  tells the reader what they are to the writer.
- **The empathy preamble.** One sentence of recognition is a kindness. A paragraph of it
  is a delay, and a reader in acute grief experiences a delay as being handled.

## 6. The reconciliation

Every technique above, restated as it survives the binding voice rules in
`docs/GUIDES_VOICE_DEBRIEF_2026-08.md`. **Where these conflict, the voice rules win.**

| Technique from the sample | How it survives here |
|---|---|
| Open on the hardest fact | Keep. This is already the Medicaid guide's opening move. |
| Open on the next action | Keep. Already the Burial Guide's opening. |
| Open on a number | Keep, with the caution that a live price must come from the price list, never a guess. |
| Open on a plain definition | Keep, stripped of adjectives. |
| Second-person "you" | Keep, and add first-person "I" and "we." Martice is a person in the room, not a narrator. The outside pieces mostly cannot do this; our guides can and should. |
| Short sentence carries the weight | Keep. Reinforces the debrief's "short sentences, plain language." |
| Fact, then footing | Keep. This is the mechanism behind "doesn't sell, informs." |
| Contrast and aside punctuation | **Changed.** The sampled writers reach for an em dash constantly. Em dashes are banned outright. Use a period and a new sentence, or a comma. This is the most frequent conflict you will hit. |
| Name the price | Keep, and it is stronger here than in any sampled piece, because we actually know our numbers. |
| Say what they don't have to buy | Keep. Unqualified. |
| Admit the trade-off | Keep. |
| Point at the source and invite checking | Keep. |
| State your standing | Keep, in first person, using **"family service director"**. Never "counselor." Full title where a title is needed: Family Service and Advanced Planning Director. |
| Explain why a charge exists | Keep. |
| Industry vocabulary for stock and supply | **Banned.** Never "inventory," in any construction. Use "all new," "available now," "not currently offered." |
| Rhetorical rhythm, imagery, anaphora | **Cut.** No literary flourish. |
| Stacked adjectives for warmth | **Cut.** One word or none. "Dignified, straightforward option" is exactly the pattern the debrief rejects. |
| Contrarian "here is what you have wrong" framing | **Cut.** See §7. |
| Adversarial stance toward the trade | **Cut.** Honesty from inside, not attack from outside. |
| Humor | **Cut** in guides. |
| Empathy preamble | Reduced to at most one sentence, and only where the reader is plainly in the middle of a death. |
| "Deeply personal decision," "peace of mind," "for generations to come" | **Banned** by the debrief. Say "there is no single right answer," or cut the sentence. |

Two additions the outside sample cannot supply, both already true of the gold-standard
guides and both binding:

- **Contractions throughout.** "It's," "you're," "don't," "we'll." The sampled consumer
  writing uses them; the sampled trade writing does not, which is most of why it sounds
  the way it does.
- **The table test.** Would Martice say this sentence to a family sitting across from
  him? If it sounds like a pamphlet, it is a pamphlet.

## 7. Banned openers

### The pattern

The live example, from `cemetery-property-guide.html`, Section 1:

> "This is the thing families most often have wrong, and it shapes nearly everything else
> in this guide."

The operator rejected this. It is banned as a class, not as a sentence.

**The class:** any opening that talks about the guide instead of the subject. Concretely,
an opener is banned if it does any of the following before saying something useful:

1. **Editorializes about the guide itself.** "This guide will explain," "what follows is,"
   "before we go any further."
2. **Ranks its own importance.** "The most important thing in this guide," "if you read
   nothing else," "this shapes everything else."
3. **Tells the reader how wrong most families are.** "Most families get this wrong,"
   "this is the thing families most often have wrong," "contrary to what you may think."
4. **Announces significance instead of delivering it.** "This is a big decision," "this
   matters more than people realize."
5. **Clears its throat.** "There are many things to consider when..." Any sentence that
   would survive being deleted.

The failure is the same in all five: the reader spends the first sentence learning about
the document rather than about their situation. It also puts the reader in the wrong seat.
Telling someone they are probably wrong is a move that works in journalism, where the
writer is a stranger with a thesis, and fails at a table, where the writer is the person
they came to for help.

The test is mechanical. **Delete the first sentence. If the guide is unharmed, the
sentence was banned.**

### Rewritten openings

Real subject matter from real guides. Each states the useful thing first, uses
contractions, carries no em dash, and would survive the delete-the-first-sentence test.

**1. Cemetery Property Guide, Section 1 (replacing the banned live example):**

> When you buy cemetery property, you're not buying land. You're buying the exclusive
> right to use one particular space as a resting place, and the document you get says
> exactly that. It's called a Certificate of Interment Rights.

**2. Burial Vault Guide (currently opens in manufacturer-brochure voice):**

> A burial vault is a concrete container that goes into the grave, and the casket goes
> inside it. It keeps the ground from settling over the years, which is why the cemetery
> requires one. Here's what each one is made of and what it costs.

**3. Outside Marker Installation Rules (currently opens as a compliance memo):**

> You're welcome to buy a marker somewhere other than from us. A few extra fees come with
> that, and I'd rather you have the numbers before you order anything. Here they are, and
> here's what each one pays for.

**4. Scattering Garden Options (currently opens in flowery marketing voice):**

> In a scattering garden, the cremated remains are scattered into the garden itself.
> Your loved one's name is engraved on a granite memorial in the garden. That's the
> place your family comes back to.

(Corrected at director audit 2026-08-20: the first draft claimed "there's no individual
space to buy," but every package on that page itemizes a Scattering Space purchase.
Example openings are style models, never fact sources — verify every fact against the
live guide before importing a sentence.)

## 8. Calibration against the gold standard

Openings read on 2026-08-20 from `medicaid-family-guide.html`, `who-decides-guide.html`,
`cremation-or-burial-guide.html`, `veterans-guide.html`, `burial-guide.html`, and
`cemetery-property-guide.html`. The debrief names the first five (with the Cemetery
Property guide) as the model. That holds, with one exception noted below.

**What they already do, and what the rewrite tracks must preserve:**

| Technique | Where it already lives |
|---|---|
| Open on the hardest fact | **Medicaid.** It opens on someone going into a nursing home being told to spend down their savings, then says the rules are on your side here. Hard situation first, footing second. |
| Open on the next action | **Burial Guide.** "Call us first," a phone number, and a note that under hospice the nurse calls for you. Nothing precedes it. |
| Open on the decision itself, plainly | **Who Decides.** Someone has to sign for a cremation and Washington law is specific about who. Subject, stakes, no preamble. |
| Refuse to steer | **Cremation or Burial.** Says outright it does not push you toward either and there's no wrong answer. This is trust technique #3 executed better than anything in the outside sample. |
| Say what's given and what's still owed | **Veterans.** States that the space is a gift, that none of the VA benefits are automatic, and that a family should still expect charges. Gift and bill in the same breath. |
| Point at the source, invite checking | **Who Decides.** Names RCW 68.50.160, explains what RCW means, and tells the reader they're welcome to read it and check us. |
| Define inline, no jargon left standing | **Cemetery Property** on Certificate of Interment Rights; **Who Decides** on RCW. |
| Name every charge | **Cemetery Property**, which promises every charge in an interment and what each one is for. |
| Short lead sentence, then the detail | All six. The pull line at the top of each guide is deliberately short. |

**The exception, and the direction of travel.** `cemetery-property-guide.html` is listed
in the debrief as a gold-standard guide and its top-of-page opening is fine. Its
Section 1 body then opens with the banned sentence. So the gold standard is not
uniformly clean, and a track must not assume a guide is safe because the debrief lists it
as good. Check openings section by section.

**Direction of travel:** rewrites move guides TOWARD the register above, never away from
it. If a rewritten paragraph is warmer, more elegant, or more moving than the Medicaid
guide, it is wrong. The target is the Medicaid guide's second sentence, which explains
how the rules work in plain words and tells the reader who they should really be asking.
