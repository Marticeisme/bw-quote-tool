Understanding Burial Vaults

What Is a Burial Vault?

A burial vault is a concrete container that goes into the grave, and the casket goes inside it. It's lined and sealed to resist water, and it carries the weight of the earth above it along with the mowers and backhoes that pass over the grave. It's also what stops the ground settling into a dip. That's why the cemetery asks for one.

The vault is one of several charges beyond the cemetery space itself. The Cemetery Property Guide explains how it fits with the space, opening and closing, endowment care, and the rest.

They're made of high-strength concrete with a metal or plastic liner inside.

If it isn't lined and sealed, it isn't a burial vault.

The other thing you'll hear about is an outer burial container, sometimes called a gravebox. That's a plain concrete box with drainage holes in it, so ground water runs straight through. It holds the ground up, but it doesn't keep water out, and it isn't lined or sealed. That's the whole difference between the two.

Understanding Protection

Three Levels of Vault Construction

Wilbert makes them in three levels: Basic, Standard and Premium. All three are reinforced concrete. What changes is how much reinforcement there is and what the liner is made of.

Premium

Bronze or Copper

Double- or triple-reinforced, with a bronze or copper liner. The Wilbert Bronze has bronze outside too.
