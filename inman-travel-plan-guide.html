Section 1

Why This Exists

The bill nobody plans for

Almost every arrangement I help with assumes the death happens here. Most of them do. The ones that don't are the ones that go badly.

Think about who in your family is regularly a long way from home. The winter in Arizona. The cruise. The trip back to the Philippines, or Vietnam, or Mexico, to see everybody. A daughter working overseas. A husband who still drives to Montana every fall.

When someone dies in one of those places, the family is on the phone within hours with a funeral home they have never heard of, in a town they may not know, sometimes in another language and another currency. They have to decide fast, they have no idea what anything should cost, and they are doing it in the worst week of their lives.

And the bill for just getting their person home runs $1,500 to $22,000. That is before a single dollar of the funeral back here.

$499, paid one time, covers it for the rest of your life. When it happens, the family makes one call to Inman and it is handled and paid for. That is the whole thing. Everything else on this page is detail.

Where a credit card or a travel policy falls short

Families often tell me they already have something like this through a credit card or a travel policy. Usually they don't, and the differences are the ones that matter most to the people most likely to need it.

  • No health questionsThere is no medical form and nobody is turned down for their health. A policy will often do both.

Section 10

Details Worth Knowing

The fine print, in plain words

None of this is hidden, but it is the kind of thing families find out at the wrong moment, so here it is up front.

  • What counts as homeThe plan works off your legal residence, meaning the fixed, full-time home you actually live in. An address you have lived at for 180 days or more in the twelve months before death counts as your legal residence. That includes a nursing home: 180 days there and the nursing home is home.
  • Enrolling while you are already awayIf you sign up while you are out of town, the plan takes effect after you get home, and it covers your travel from then on. It won't cover the trip you are already on.
  • Medical tourism isn't coveredTraveling somewhere specifically for a medical procedure sits outside the plan.
  • Going overseasCoverage is good for unlimited international travel. Inman asks that you give their Service Center a call before an international trip. It costs nothing and it makes their side faster if anything happens.
  • What arrives in the mailA welcome letter, a wallet-size member ID card, and a change of address card. Keep the ID card in your wallet, because that is where somebody will look.

Common Questions

What people ask me about this one

How is the 75 miles measured?

From your legal residence, the full-time home you live in. Die 75 miles or more from it, or in another country, and the plan applies. Closer to home than that and we handle it the ordinary way.

Does this replace a funeral plan?

No. This gets a person home. What happens after that, the service, the casket or urn, the cemetery property, is separate. Plenty of families do both, and the two fit together.

We already own property at the cemetery. Does that change anything?

It makes this more useful, not less. Your space is here and your person may not be. Getting them back to it is exactly what this covers.

Doesn't my travel policy or credit card already do this?

Usually not, and the exclusions tend to land on the people most likely to need it. Check the age limit, the health questions, whether it expires, and whether you have to sign up before every trip. This one has none of those.

Is there a health questionnaire?

No. Any age is accepted and nobody is turned down for their health.

Can I buy it for my parents?

Yes. You can enroll anyone, and a lot of people buy this for a parent who travels or spends the winter somewhere else.

What if we move?

Tell Inman. They send a change of address card with the welcome packet for exactly this reason. Your legal residence is what the 75 miles is measured from, so keeping it current matters.

Does it cover a death here at home?

No, and it isn't meant to. Under 75 miles is a local death, and that's the arrangement I would be handling anyway.

Is there a cost to ask you about it?

No. A conversation is free and carries no obligation. If I don't think it is worth it for your family, I'll say so.
