The three pieces

An upright headstone is built in layers. Each one has a job.

Tablet

The upright stone that carries the name, the dates and the design. You'll also hear it called the die. It sits on the base and is pinned or set so it stays put.

Base

The granite block the tablet stands on. It's wider and deeper than the tablet, so the headstone is steady and looks finished from every side.

Foundation

A 4-inch slab of Barrell Gray granite under the base, set level with the lawn. It's a little wider and longer than the base and keeps the headstone from settling or shifting.

Single or companion. A single upright is for one person. A companion upright is one wide tablet, 36 or 48 inches across, with room for two names, so a couple shares one headstone on a longer base. The Compare sizes view on the Design tab draws every size side by side at the same scale.

Uprights here are made by Pacific Coast Memorials, and they're all Group 2 granite. Every color on the Design tab is the same price for the same size.

Finishes, and what they look like

Granite can be finished three ways. The finish changes how the stone catches the light, so it's one of the easiest differences to see.

Photos of blank stones from Design Memorials (Pacific Coast Memorials).

Monument makers write the finish as a short code. Here's what each one means on a tablet:

CodeWhat's polishedThe restPrice
P2Front and backTop and sides rock pitchedIncluded
P3Front, back and topSides rock pitchedAdds $770
P5Every side but the bottom (all polished)Nothing left roughAdds $960

The base comes with a polished top and rock-pitched sides. A polished margin adds a smooth 2-inch polished band along the top edges of the base, so the rough sides get a clean frame. That adds $1,060.

The foundation has a polished top and sawn sides. Rock-pitched sides on the foundation add 10% to the foundation price.

Top shapes

Serpentine, oval and flat tops are all the same price. A custom shape is quoted separately.

  • Serpentine curves up from the shoulders into a rounded top. It's the most common upright shape.
  • Oval is a gentle arc that drops about 2 inches at the ends.
  • Flat is straight across.

Vases

The vases I quote for uprights are Paragon metal vases, $685 each. They sit on the foundation in front of the base, one at each end, or just one if you'd like. Non-permanent vases aren't allowed in the park, so a vase you'll keep using has to be one we install.

What the price includes

  • The tablet and base in the granite, size and finish you pick
  • Any standard design or emblem from the design book
  • The names, the dates and one line of epitaph
  • Your proof, plus two rounds of changes
  • The foundation and the installation, each on its own line
Nothing gets made until you've approved the proof. The proof is a drawing of your headstone with the lettering laid out, so you see exactly what will be cut before the stone is made.

What you choose later, at added cost

These get settled on the proof. I'll price each one when you pick it.

  • More lines of epitaph, or lettering and a design on the back
  • Shape carving, where part of the design is carved so it stands out from the stone
  • Etching a portrait or a scene into the stone. Laser and hand diamond etching are done on Absolute Black.
  • A ceramic photo, color or black and white, set into the tablet

Where uprights go

Uprights are allowed in Section 6, in the upright blocks of Section 23 and in Cascade Court. Before we order, the superintendent checks the space so we know it can hold the headstone.

The warranty

Pacific Coast Memorials replaces any part of the stone that cracks or breaks down from weather, and fixes its own mistakes at no charge. It doesn't cover vandalism, earthquakes, floods or wind, so it's worth adding the headstone to your homeowner's insurance.
