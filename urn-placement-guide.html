SECTION 1

What Is a Columbarium?

Where an urn goes when it is placed above ground

A columbarium is a structure full of individual compartments, called niches, each one built to hold urns. It is one of the three ways to place cremated remains here. The other two are burying the urn in the ground and scattering.

Placement carries charges beyond the niche or garden space itself. The Cemetery Property Guide walks through the space, endowment care, opening and closing, and the rest.

Each niche is sealed with a granite or bronze faceplate that can be engraved with the name, dates, and a personal inscription. Every niche at Washington Memorial Park carries the right to place two urns. There is no separate single-urn niche here. The one exception is the Veterans Court of Honor, where each niche holds one.

Washington Memorial Park has six columbarium settings. They are shown below with what each one costs; you are welcome to walk any of them during park hours.

Both carry the same charges beyond the space itself: endowment care, opening and closing, the recording fee, and a flush marker quoted separately. Ask us to price the number of placements you have in mind.

WHAT THE URN ITSELF HAS TO BE

Anything buried in the ground has to survive being there. Either choice satisfies it:

  • Put the urn inside an urn vault, a small outer container that protects it underground, so a wood or ceramic urn can still be buried. See the Burial Vault Guide.
  • Or choose an urn rated for burial. The marble urns in the Cremation Guide and the Urn Catalog go straight into the ground.
